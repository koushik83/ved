Raksha Bandhan falls on Shravana Purnima: the day is the one whose forenoon Purnima holds, or holds more of where it touches two. The first half of Purnima is Bhadra, which tradition avoids, so rakhi is tied after Bhadra ends and while Purnima lasts, in daylight or in the evening hours after sunset. Purnima begins and ends at one instant worldwide, but each city's day is its own, so the date and the time differ between India and a city abroad.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Raksha Bandhan on a different date in New Jersey than in India?

The day is set by where Purnima falls in each city's own day. For a city many hours behind India, Purnima can hold the forenoon of the earlier date, so the festival comes a day before India's.

What is Bhadra and why is it avoided?

Bhadra is Vishti karana, the first half of Purnima. Tradition holds it unlucky for tying rakhi, so the time given starts when it ends.

Can rakhi be tied after sunset?

Yes, in the evening hours after sunset (pradosh kaal) while Purnima lasts, which the time given includes when Bhadra ends late in the day.
