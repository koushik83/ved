Hindu calendar 2026–2027 for Australia

Hindu festivals and fasts are set by the tithi in force at the time of day each tradition names: sunrise for most fasts, moonrise for Sankashti, the evening for Diwali and Karwa Chauth, midnight for Maha Shivratri. The tithi changes at one instant worldwide, but each city meets it at its own hour, so a date in Australia can differ from India's and sometimes between its cities.

The table gives each date for Sydney, Melbourne, Brisbane, Adelaide and Perth, worked out for each city's own sunrise, sunset and moonrise. A date in bold differs from the first column. Ekadashi is the Smarta date; where the Vaishnava date differs it has its own row. Every city below has its own page with the times.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is a festival on a different date in Australia than in India?

The deciding moment, a sunrise, a moonrise or an evening, comes to each city at a different point in the tithi. When the tithi begins or ends close to that moment, the date moves by a day.

Which date should I follow if I live between two of these cities?

The nearest city's, or your own city's page if it has one. The date differs between cities only when the deciding moment falls close to the local sunrise, sunset or moonrise.
