Holika Dahan, the bonfire, is lit on the evening of Phalguna Purnima, in pradosh kaal after sunset while Purnima is in force, and Holi, the day of colours, is the next day. The first half of Purnima is Bhadra, which tradition avoids for the bonfire, so it is lit after Bhadra ends. Purnima begins and ends at one instant worldwide, but sunset is local, so the date and the time can differ between India and a city abroad.

Where Purnima is in force in both evenings, the evening it covers more of is taken. Where Bhadra ends after pradosh kaal but before midnight, the time is given from the end of Bhadra. Where it lasts past midnight, tradition lights the fire in the tail of Bhadra or in pradosh kaal, and practice varies, so follow your temple's time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is Holi on a different date in New Jersey than in India?

The bonfire belongs to the evening, and the evening comes to each city at a different point in the Purnima tithi. When Purnima begins after sunset in one city and before it in another, the date differs, and Holi, the next day, moves with it.

What is Bhadra and why is it avoided?

Bhadra is Vishti karana, which falls in the first half of Purnima. Tradition holds it unlucky for beginnings, so the bonfire is lit after it ends. When it runs late into the night, communities follow the time their temple gives.

Which day are colours played?

The morning after Holika Dahan. In many places the day is also called Dhulandi or Rangwali Holi.
