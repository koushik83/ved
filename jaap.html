What Is Naam Jaap?

Naam jaap (नाम जप) is the repetition of a divine name or mantra, such as Ram, said quietly or aloud with attention. Devotees traditionally count the repetitions on a japa mala of 108 beads, moving one bead for each name. This jaap counter does the same work on your phone: one tap is one bead, and after 108 taps one mala is complete.

Why 108?

A japa mala has 108 beads and one guru bead, the sumeru, which marks the start and end of a round. The number 108 is held sacred in Hindu tradition, and one full round of the mala is counted as 108 repetitions. Counting in malas makes it easy to keep a daily sankalp, such as one, five or eleven malas.

Ram Naam Jaap

Ram Naam jaap is the most widely practised naam jaap among devotees of Shri Ram. In Shri Ramcharitmanas, Goswami Tulsidas describes the name of Ram as a jewel-lamp placed at the doorway of the tongue, bringing light within and without. Many devotees chant राम or the full taraka mantra, श्री राम जय राम जय जय राम, and count their malas each day. For guidance from Shri Ram on a question, see the Ram Shalaka Prashnavali.
