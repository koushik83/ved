# Timeline of Sai Baba's Life, 1838 to 1918 | Shirdi Sai Baba

Source: https://vedprashnavali.com/shirdisaibaba

## About This Timeline

Almost everything known about Sai Baba's life comes from the people who lived around him in Shirdi and wrote down what they saw, above all in the Shri Sai Satcharitra. Baba himself said almost nothing about where he came from. The dates below are the ones devotees have kept; the earliest are traditional rather than recorded, and we say so where that is the case. For the stories behind the events, see [Sai Baba's life and stories](https://vedprashnavali.com/sai-baba-ask-questions-answers).

## Early Life

### 1838, by tradition: birth

Sai Baba is traditionally said to have been born on 28 September 1838 at Pathri, in the Parbhani district of Maharashtra. No record of his parents or his birth name survives, and he never confirmed any account of his origins.

### Youth: years with a teacher

Baba spoke of spending years in the service of a guru, whom he never named. Some devotees believed the teacher to have been a Sufi fakir, others a Hindu sage of Selu. Baba kept a lamp burning at a spot under the neem tree in Shirdi, which he said marked the resting place of his teacher.

### Around 1854: a youth under the neem tree

Villagers of Shirdi remembered a boy of about sixteen who sat in meditation under the neem tree, untroubled by heat or cold, and who went away after some months as quietly as he had come.

## Life in Shirdi

### 1858: the return

Baba came back to Shirdi with the wedding party of Chand Patil, a Muslim headman of the village of Dhoop. The priest of the Khandoba temple, Mhalsapati, greeted the young fakir with the words "Ya Sai", welcome Sai, and the name stayed with him for the rest of his life. From then on he never left Shirdi for long.

### 1886: the three-day samadhi

Suffering from a severe attack of asthma, Baba told Mhalsapati that he would leave his body and asked him to guard it for three days. The villagers wanted to bury him. Mhalsapati refused, and on the third day Baba stirred and returned to life.

### 1890s: the first devotees from outside

Nanasaheb Chandorkar, a deputy collector, came to Shirdi in the early 1890s and became one of Baba's closest devotees, bringing many others with him. Around 1897 Gopalrao Gund, a police official, started the annual Urs fair in Shirdi in gratitude for the birth of a son.

### 1898: the sleeping plank

For a time Baba slept on a narrow plank hung from the rafters of the mosque by strips of old cloth, with lamps burning at each end. No one ever saw him climb up or down.

### Early 1900s: teaching and the handi

Baba explained a verse of the Bhagavad Gita to Chandorkar with a learning that astonished him. In these years he also cooked for the gathering himself in great pots, the handis, a practice that ended around 1910 when devotees began bringing food in plenty.

### 1908: formal worship begins

On Guru Purnima, at Baba's own prompting, devotees began worshipping him formally as their guru. Soon after, the Chavadi procession began: on alternate nights Baba slept in the Chavadi, the village hall, and devotees walked him there with lamps, music and an umbrella, a ceremony still re-enacted in Shirdi.

## The Years of Fame

### 1910: the arm in the fire

Baba thrust his arm into the dhuni, the sacred fire, and held it there until he was pulled back. He said he had saved a blacksmith's child who had slipped towards a furnace far away. He refused treatment, and the burn was dressed only with ghee by his attendant Bhagoji Shinde.

### 1910: Dixit Wada and the growing village

The foundation of Dixit Wada was laid in December 1910, the second large lodging built for visiting devotees after Sathe Wada. Shirdi was changing from a village into a place of pilgrimage. In September of the same year Baba grieved openly at the passing of Gajanan Maharaj of Shegaon, calling him "my Gajanan".

### 1911 to 1912: the plague and the Satcharitra

When plague came to Shirdi, Baba ground wheat in a hand mill and had the flour scattered at the edge of the village. It was this sight that moved Govind Dabholkar, Hemadpant, to want to write Baba's life. In January 1912 Baba cured Dadasaheb Khaparde's son of plague, and Kashinath Upasani composed the Sai Mahimna Stotra, still sung at the Shirdi aarti. In the same year the Ram Navami festival began to be celebrated together with the Urs.

### 1916: the Simollanghan

On Vijayadashami, the day of crossing the boundary, Baba tore off his kafni, threw it into the fire and said that this was his own Simollanghan. Devotees later understood it as a sign of the day on which he would leave.

### 1916: Hemadpant's permission

Hemadpant asked Baba's leave to write the Satcharitra and received it with his blessing. The book was written in Marathi over the following years and published in 1930.

## The Last Days

### 28 September 1918: the fever

Baba developed a fever and from then on ate little. A tiger brought to the mosque by wandering performers died quietly at his feet, an event devotees count among his last miracles. He asked a devotee to read Ramvijay to him, and listened for fourteen days.

### 15 October 1918: Mahasamadhi

On the afternoon of Vijayadashami, a Tuesday, Baba gave nine rupees to Laxmibai Shinde, asked to be taken to the Wada, and passed away leaning against Bayaji Appa Kote Patil. That morning Das Ganu, in Pandharpur, had dreamed that Baba came to tell him Dwarkamai had fallen and asked him to come and cover his body with flowers.

### 16 October 1918: the Samadhi

After a day of discussion between Hindu and Muslim devotees, Baba's body was carried in procession through the village and laid to rest in the Buti Wada, the stone building Baba had himself blessed. It is now the Samadhi Mandir, the centre of the Shirdi temple.

### 27 October 1918: the thirteenth day

Devotees gathered from far and near for the thirteenth-day rites, and a feast was given to Brahmins and to the poor.

## After 1918

Before he passed, Baba had promised that he would remain active from his tomb and answer those who called on him. Devotees across India and abroad report that promise kept. The Satcharitra was published in 1930, the Shree Saibaba Sansthan Trust now cares for the temple, and Shirdi receives tens of thousands of pilgrims a day. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### When was Sai Baba born?

By tradition on 28 September 1838 at Pathri in Maharashtra. There is no record, and Baba never confirmed it.

### When did Sai Baba settle in Shirdi?

In 1858, when he returned with Chand Patil's wedding party. He had been seen in the village once before, as a youth of about sixteen.

### How long did Sai Baba live in Shirdi?

About sixty years, from 1858 until his passing on 15 October 1918.

### When did Sai Baba take Mahasamadhi?

On Tuesday 15 October 1918, Vijayadashami, in the afternoon. He was laid to rest the next day in the Buti Wada, now the Samadhi Mandir.

### Who wrote the Sai Satcharitra and when?

Govind Raghunath Dabholkar, known as Hemadpant, with Baba's permission given in 1916. It was published in Marathi in 1930.
