## The Eleven Assurances

Devotees of Sai Baba keep a list of eleven promises drawn from his sayings in the last years of his life. They are painted on the walls of Shirdi and read at the start of many Sai gatherings. In plain words, Baba promised:

The assurances are read as a whole: they ask for faith and surrender, and they promise help in return. The Sai Baba Prashnavali on this site rests on the seventh and tenth of them, the promise that Baba answers those who ask.

## What Baba Taught

### Sabka Malik Ek: one master of all

Baba's most repeated saying was that everyone's master is one. He would not be claimed by Hindus or Muslims alone, kept both their observances, and taught that the name used for God does not divide those who call on him.

### Shraddha and saburi: faith and patience

Asked what he wanted from a devotee, Baba named two things: faith, and the patience to wait for faith to bear fruit. He said that if a devotee gave him these two, he would do the rest.

### Charity and dakshina

Baba asked for dakshina, a small gift of money, from most visitors, and gave every coin away by nightfall. He used the asking to teach giving: a person who parts with a little, cheerfully, loosens the hold that money has on the mind. He fed the hungry, including dogs and other animals, and told devotees that whoever feeds a hungry creature feeds him.

### The guru

Baba spoke of his own guru with reverence and taught that a sincere seeker needs a guide. He warned against running after mantras and miracles, and said that steady devotion to one's chosen deity or guru was worth more than any technique.

### Kindness and restraint

Baba would not allow a snake or a scorpion to be killed in his presence if it could be sent away, and he asked devotees to answer anger with calm. Speak gently, he said, and let no one leave your door hungry or insulted.

### Contentment and duty

Baba told householders to do their work well, live simply, keep their word, and leave the result to God. He did not ask his devotees to renounce the world; he asked them to live in it honestly and remember him.

## How Devotees Worship Sai Baba

### Thursday

Thursday, the day of the guru, is Baba's day. Devotees visit a Sai temple, keep a fast or eat simply, read the Satcharitra, and offer flowers, a garland and a sweet before his picture. Many begin a vow of nine Thursdays for a particular wish, reading his life and feeding others each week.

### The four aartis

At Shirdi the aarti is sung four times a day: Kakad aarti at dawn, the midday aarti, Dhoop aarti at sunset and Shej aarti at night before Baba is put to rest. Devotees at home usually sing one, morning or evening, before a lamp and incense.

### Udi

Udi is the ash of the dhuni, the fire Baba kept burning in Dwarkamai, which still burns today. A pinch is applied to the forehead, or taken with water, as Baba's blessing and protection. It is given freely at Shirdi and sent by post to devotees everywhere.

### Reading the Satcharitra

The Shri Sai Satcharitra is read as an act of worship, often as a parayan over seven days beginning on a Thursday, and often with a particular prayer in mind. Baba promised that whoever reads his life with love will be freed of their troubles.

### Naam jaap

Repeating Om Sai Ram, or simply Sai, on a mala of 108 is the simplest daily practice. The [Naam Jaap Counter](https://vedprashnavali.com/jaap) on this site counts the malas for you.

## A Sai Shrine at Home

Nothing elaborate is needed. Devotees keep a picture or a small image of Baba in a clean, quiet place, facing east or north, with a lamp, incense, a few flowers and a small bowl of udi. The daily worship is short: light the lamp, offer the flowers and a little food, sing or read an aarti, and sit for a few minutes in his company. On Thursday the offering is a little larger and the food is shared with others afterwards.

Baba himself cared nothing for ceremony. The worship that pleases him, devotees say, is a kept word, a shared meal and a mind that remembers him through the day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are Sai Baba's eleven assurances?

Eleven promises drawn from Baba's sayings, from the ending of troubles for those who come to Shirdi to the promise that there will be no want in a devotee's house. They are listed in full above.

### What did Sai Baba mean by shraddha and saburi?

Faith and patience. Baba said these two were all he asked of a devotee, and that he would do the rest.

### Why is Thursday Sai Baba's day?

Thursday is traditionally the day of the guru, and Baba's devotees regard him as their guru. Temples are fullest on Thursday, and the nine-Thursday vow is kept on that day.

### What is udi?

The ash of the dhuni, the sacred fire in Dwarkamai. Baba gave it to everyone who came, and devotees apply it to the forehead as his blessing.

### How do I start worshipping Sai Baba at home?

Keep a picture of Baba in a clean place, light a lamp and incense each day, offer a flower and a little food, and read or sing an aarti. Thursday is the day for a longer worship.
